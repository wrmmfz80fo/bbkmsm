# How an Unexpected Diagnosis Changed One Man’s Life

David Smith’s* oldest daughter Misha was the first person to notice that he had a small spot just underneath his left eye, that was only about the size of an eraser head on a pencil. As he explains, it didn’t look like much of anything, but it was there, and it was a prominent spot.

At his daughter’s and his wife’s encouragement, he made an appointment to have it examined by a dermatologist, who removed the small patch of skin and sent it to be tested. The doctor didn’t seem worried, so David left the appointment with little concern about what the results would be. “My doctor doubted that it was any kind of cancer, so I walked away from that appointment thinking I avoided that,” he says.

The doctor wanted to see him the following week, and initially David didn’t think much of it. “But as soon as the dermatologist came into the exam room, I knew something was wrong,” he says. That’s when he found out he had cancer.

Deciding on a Treatment Plan

David then underwent additional testing and imaging to help inform his treatment plan, which revealed the cancer had metastasized—or spread—into his lungs and lymph nodes. He was ultimately diagnosed with advanced melanoma, which historically has been associated with a challenging prognosis.

David’s doctor talked through treatment options with him, which he also discussed and researched with his family in order to make as informed a decision as possible. David and his doctor ultimately chose OpdualagTM (nivolumab and relatlimab-rmbw), a first-of-its-kind immunotherapy combination treatment made up of nivolumab, a PD-1 inhibitor, and relatlimab, a LAG-3 blocking antibody.

Opdualag is approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to treat adults and children 12 years of age or older with a type of skin cancer called melanoma that has spread or cannot be removed by surgery (advanced melanoma). It is not known if Opdualag is safe and effective when used in children 12 years of age or older who weigh less than 88 pounds (40 kg), or in children younger than 12 years of age.

Opdualag may not work for everyone. Individual results may vary. Patients should speak with their healthcare provider to learn more.

Opdualag can cause serious side effects anytime during and after treatment. The most serious side effects include: immune system-related side effects; severe infusion reactions and complications with donor stem cell (allogeneic) transplants. The most common side effects include: muscle and bone pain; tiredness; decreased red blood cell and white blood cell counts; increased liver function test results; rash; itching, diarrhea; decreased salt (sodium) in your blood. Immune system-related side effects may include: lung problems; intestinal problems; liver problems; hormone gland problems; kidney problems; skin problems; heart problems; problems in other organs and tissues. Please see additional Important Safety and Prescribing Information below.
