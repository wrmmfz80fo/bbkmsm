What Is a Beer Belly, Anyway?

A BEER BELLY is kind of a misnomer to begin with. “What people are talking about when they use the term ‘beer belly’ is essentially just excess weight around the abdomen,” says Jessica Bartfield, MD, clinical associate professor of weight management in the Department of Surgery at Wake Forest University School of Medicine.

Some people are more likely to acquire a beer belly than others. “Genetics is a strong predictor of where we gain and lose weight, and often people have patterns of losing weight in one area and regaining it in the same area. Age, hormones, physical activity and medications can change this pattern,” says David Creel, PhD, of the Endocrinology and Metabolism Institute at the Cleveland Clinic.

In addition, men and postmenopausal women tend to be more likely to carry that weight around the middle than to have it show up subcutaneously or more peripherally.

So basically, extra weight around the middle isn’t really beer’s fault (but back to that in a minute). What doctors don’t love about this distribution of weight, no matter what’s putting it there, is that it’s associated with things that damage your health, including type 2 diabetes, insulin resistance, high blood pressure, increased cardiovascular disease risk and even a higher likelihood of sleep apnea. (You’ll often hear a cluster of these problems referred to as “metabolic dysfunction” or “metabolic disease.”) Weight in the abdomen can also be associated with negative changes in your liver.

So Can Non-Alcoholic Beer Make You Gain Weight?

OF COURSE IT can, if it contributes to your taking in more calories than you need. If you’re drinking down a 6-pack every night because you can and still feel fine at work the next day, you’re also taking in nearly 400 calories.

While many NA beers contain fewer calories than their leaded counterparts—about 60 to 70 calories in a 12-oz 0.0 compared to 90 to 100 in the Light versions; 140ish in the regular—it can still add up.

Can 0.0 Help You Lose Weight?

YOUR ABILITY TO drink these and lose weight depends on how you use them, but there are reasons they could help you out. First of all, “they don’t necessarily have the higher sugar content other drinks do that can stimulate overeating or wanting to overconsume,” says Dr. Bartfield.

And second, you don’t have the inhibition loss you have with alcohol—the situation that can drive you to drop your usual eating plan and have whatever looks good, sounds good, or is in front of you.

Third, you may sleep better on NA than you would if your drinks are spiked. The sleep disruptions that alcohol is famous for (makes you feel sleepy at first, tends to disturb sleep later in the night) “can contribute to changes in appetite and changes in energy. You may not even move as much the following day,” Dr. Bartfield says.
