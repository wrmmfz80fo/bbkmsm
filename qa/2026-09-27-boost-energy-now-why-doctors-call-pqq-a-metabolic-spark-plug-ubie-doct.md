What Is PQQ?

PQQ (pyrroloquinoline quinone) is a small quinone molecule found in soil, plants, certain foods, and human breast milk. Unlike classic vitamins, PQQ acts more like a coenzyme or growth factor, supporting critical cellular functions rather than serving as a direct nutrient.

Key points:

Discovered in 1979 as a bacterial cofactor

Naturally present in kiwifruit, soybeans, spinach, and green peppers

How PQQ Fuels Mitochondrial Health

Mitochondria are known as the "powerhouses" of our cells. They convert the nutrients we consume into adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the main energy currency. When mitochondrial function declines, you may feel fatigued, foggy, or run down.

PQQ earns its "metabolic spark plug" reputation by:

Protecting mitochondria from oxidative stress• Scavenges free radicals• Maintains membrane integrity for efficient energy production

Supporting enzyme systems in energy pathways• Enhances activity of NADH dehydrogenase (Complex I)• Improves electron transport chain efficiency

Together, these effects help optimize your body's ability to generate ATP, translating into smoother, more reliable energy throughout the day.

Scientific Evidence Behind PQQ and Energy

Research on PQQ has grown in recent years. Here's a snapshot of key findings:

Human trial (2016): Healthy adults taking 20 mg PQQ daily for 12 weeks reported a 17% improvement in energy, focus, and overall well-being versus placebo.

Animal studies: Rats given PQQ showed enhanced endurance in treadmill tests and increased expression of genes tied to mitochondrial function.

Cognitive benefits: Pilot studies suggest PQQ may support memory and mental clarity, likely through the same mitochondrial mechanisms that boost physical energy.

While more large-scale human trials are needed, existing data are encouraging for anyone seeking PQQ for daily energy levels.

Recommended Dosage and Timing

Most studies have used doses between 10–20 mg of PQQ per day. Here are practical tips:

Start low: 5 mg once daily for the first week to assess tolerance

Typical range: 10–20 mg per day, taken with or after a meal for better absorption

Avoid high doses (>40 mg) without medical supervision—research on long-term safety at very high levels is limited

Pairing PQQ with CoQ10 may produce synergistic benefits, since both support mitochondrial health through complementary pathways. Talk to your doctor before combining supplements.

Safety and Potential Side Effects

PQQ is generally well tolerated, but you should be aware of:

Mild digestive upset: nausea or stomach discomfort in sensitive individuals

Headache or jitteriness (rare)

Possible interaction with medications that affect mitochondrial function (e.g., certain chemotherapy agents)

To minimize risk:

Choose a high-quality, third-party tested PQQ supplement

Follow dosing guidelines closely

Keep an eye on any unusual symptoms and report them to your healthcare provider
