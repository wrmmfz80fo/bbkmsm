TODAY, SENATORS RUBEN Gallego (D-AZ) and Roger Marshall (R-KS) made a move they’re hoping can improve men’s health in the US.

This morning, they introduced the bipartisan State of Men’s Health Act in the Senate. This is the legislation that would set up an Office of Men’s Health and require a study from the Government Accountability Office on the state of men’s health. We already know that since 2016, the life span gender gap has expanded from 4.4 years to 5.9. The current average age of death for men is 73.2 years, while for women, it’s 79.1. So the office wants to figure out what’s going on with the aim of coordinating resources to figure out what to do about it.

An Office of Women’s Health already exists—it connects different offices to each other to coordinate research, education, and policy developments—and the idea is that the Office of Men’s Health would do a similar thing. The House version of the act was introduced earlier this year. We tapped Sen. Gallego—a former Marine and a champion of veteran health care and men’s mental health—to explain more about what all this might actually be able to do.

MH: What do you hope the Men’s Health Act will accomplish?

RG: There are a lot of ways we are really falling behind when it comes to men’s health in this country. Men are facing a growing health crisis and dying earlier from preventable conditions like cancer, heart disease, and mental health struggles. Instead of letting men continue to struggle alone in silence, this bill would establish an Office of Men’s Health to promote preventive care and life-saving research. When we invest in men’s health, we build stronger families and stronger communities.
