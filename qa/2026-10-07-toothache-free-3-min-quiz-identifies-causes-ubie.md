About the Symptom

Toothache describes the painful sensation in or around a tooth.

When to See a Doctor

Seek professional care if you experience any of the following symptoms

Fever

Swelling of the affected area

Possible Causes

Generally, Toothache can be related to:

Dental caries occur when the hard outer layer of your tooth, the enamel, gets destroyed, sometimes forming small holes or cavitations. Common causes include eating and drinking sugary foods, frequent snacking, dry mouth and poor oral hygiene.

A dental fistula is a pathway formed between the oral cavity and the bone surrounding the affected tooth to drain an infection. It is typically found on the gum tissue near the affected tooth. It can look like a pimple in the gums and mainly occurs after an abscess due to trauma or infection.

Inflammation of the thyroid gland which causes high levels of thyroid hormone in the body. It usually occurs after a viral infection. It presents as pain in the neck because of the swollen and inflamed thyroid gland. Can also have difficulty swallowing, hoarseness, fatigue and fever. It can also go on to cause symptoms of high thyroid hormone such as weight loss, increased bowel movements, palpitations, sweating.

Trigeminal Neuralgia

Temporomandibular Joint Osteoarthritis (TMJ)

Migraine

Shingles (Herpes Zoster)

Sinusitis

Ear Infection
