The two-time Super Bowl champ and five-time NFL MVP is adding another notch on his belt as the brand ambassador for Johnston & Murphy, the oldest continually-operating shoe company in America.

On the heels of the brand's 175th anniversary, the former Indianapolis Colts and Denver Broncos star spoke with Men's Health about his newest collaboration, which is part of what he calls his "second career." He did, however, stop short of referring to his ex-playing days as "retirement."

"To me, that's kind of the word that I sort of use—this kind of second chapter," Manning says. "Retirement, that's a different word."

Manning is a huge U.S. history buff, which makes this partnership with Johnston & Murphy work on multiple levels. His production company, Omaha Productions, has been making The Mega-Brands That Built America since 2023. The History Channel docuseries chronicles innovations of the country's most successful businesses. While Johnston & Murphy hasn't made an appearance on the show just yet, Manning wouldn't rule out a future cameo.

[Peyton Manning](https://barrierwatch.bbsxyxy.com/blog/2378416970.html)

"A cool fact—they've dressed every sitting U.S. president since the company started back in 1850," Manning says. "They've been around for a long time, and they've been consistent and relevant since their inception. It's certainly a possibility for this season."

Manning also hosts another history-adjacent series, Peyton's Places, a football-focused show that explores culturally-significant moments in the NFL. The fifth season airs this February on ESPN.

Manning says the brand approached him after he wore their Amherst shoes at the 2025 Paris Olympics. The signal-caller, though, has been a fan of Johnston & Murphy for years, and he first began wearing their shoes doing the most Manning-thing ever—shooting a commercial.

"A lot of times when you go to a commercial, they have different options of clothes and the kind of shoes to wear," Manning says. "A lot of these commercials had Johnston & Murphy shoes."

The former Walter Payton NFL Man of the Year says their shoes quickly became his go-to, thanks to their combination of style and comfort.

"Eli used to give me a hard time—he said I was getting all of my clothes from commercials," he says. "Well, if you find a good pair of shoes that fits and looks good, you stay with them."

Manning is also a big fan of the brand's quarter-zips—his go-to look on his and his brother's Monday Night Football show, the ManningCast.

Johnston & Murphy will work with Omaha Productions to shoot the 175th anniversary campaign. Manning has starred in dozens—if not hundreds—of commercials over the years. His favorite? A SportCenter bit that he filmed with his family.

"It was me, my mom and dad, and my two brothers, Cooper and Eli," he says. "We were doing the tour through the Sports Center facility, and Eli and I were messing around; we were kicking each other and giving each other wet willies. My dad turns around and gives this look, which I think anybody can relate to, from a parent, when you know you've sort of crossed the line. I think that jumps out because we filmed it in Bristol and all five of us were together, which is harder to do these days."

Much like Manning himself, his company, Omaha Productions, is staying busy. He and his brother Eli have teamed up with actor Glenn Powell for Chad Powers, a new Hulu sports comedy inspired by Eli’s viral ESPN Eli’s Places segment. That sketch, which aired three years ago, showed Eli in movie-quality makeup and prosthetics trying out for Penn State’s football team as the fictional Chad Powers. In the series, Powell plays the title role: a disgraced former college quarterback who hides his identity to suit up at a struggling school. Manning says Powell’s competitive streak is a perfect match for the original Chad Powers character.

"I mean, [Eli] went for it," Manning says of his brother in the original skit. "He ran a 40-yard dash, which is just nuts for somebody who's not playing anymore. And Glenn Powell also just went for it. This guy's a bona fide superstar, but no detail was too small for him. He hired a quarterbacks coach—he wanted his footwork to be right, he wanted the line of scrimmage mechanics to be right."

When I asked Manning for his Super Bowl pick, he admitted he’s terrible at predictions. He struggles to bet against his two AFC teams—but as a former player, he also can’t bring himself to dismiss the reigning champs.

“Until somebody knocks out the Chiefs and Eagles, they’re the defenders of each conference,” he says. “When I was playing, that’s how we looked at it—someone has to step up and take it from them.”

And what about the quarterback role itself? Manning believes it’s in good hands. While the league is full of mobile, dual-threat QBs, he doesn’t think the traditional pocket passer is going anywhere.
