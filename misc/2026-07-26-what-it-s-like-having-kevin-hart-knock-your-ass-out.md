# What It's Like Having Kevin Hart Knock Your Ass Out

WHAM!

The ball slammed into the wall above me at 60 mph, rattling plexiglass and sending my fight-or-flight instincts into overdrive as I stepped to the next foothold. I had no time to think — the very next moment, a second shot nailed me square in the head and I tumbled to the mat below.

I heard unrestrained cackling from the tower above me. Kevin Hart was reveling in his victory.

We were on the set of TKO: Total Knock Out, the latest of Hart's many projects. The comedian serves as the host of the new 10-part TV series, a mashup of Wipeout and American Gladiators with a competitive streak straight out of a reality show: one contestant runs through a four-station obstacle course while four opponents aim to pummel them with projectiles to knock them out of the running. After all five players run the course, the person with the lowest time takes home bragging rights and a $50,000 prize.

I got an early look at the set — and a run through the course — before the show's July 11 premiere after a day of shooting. The studio audience and competitors were long gone by then, so it was just me, Kevin Hart, and a camera crew to capture my stab at TKO glory.

But before we finish my tale of obstacle course hardship at the hands of one of the biggest comedians in the world, it would probably help to understand exactly why this show could become one of the surprise hits of the summer.

There Are No Friends in TKO

Hart might spend his time out of the spotlight perfecting his boxing moves and running marathons, but that doesn't mean TKO is only for high level athletes, like the course. Instead, the four combat zones have more basic obstacles: Zone 1 is a sheer wall with untrustworthy footholds above a mat, Zone 2 a shaky bridge over a ball pit, Zone 3 is populated with unsteady platforms amidst foam cubes, and Zone 4 is a rotating path across water. As someone who spent most of my run hoisting myself out of the pits below, I can attest that the falls don't hurt — but there's a desperate drive to get back up and make it through the zone without descending into the depths again.

On this course, besting your opponents — and their projectiles — reigns supreme.

The Ball Blaster at Zone 1 shoots porous yellow death spheres 60 mph, giving players a chance to line up head shots on their opponents. I felt that pain when Hart nailed me three times. Zone 2's Frisbooms are massive discs of doom, like mutated frisbees after a radioactive incident. Giant foam spike balls are catapulted out of the Spike Launcher at the pull of a lever at Zone 3. It's hammer time at Zone 4, where competitors use an oversized mallet to spring the swinging smashers of the Slammer, which can crush runners right into the water below.

"We wanted to do a show that everyone could feel like they could do and have fun with," Hart said. "This isn't about being in shape. You're dodging all types of objects that are being thrown at you, and the beauty of it is it's your competitors doing the throwing. It's a lot of fun, but also the people that are here are basically responsible for each other."

The Course By the Numbers

My run through the TKO course can be best summed up by the numbers:

Number of successes by me: -1

Number of headshots by Kevin Hart: 3

Number of swinging mallets: 4

Number of unforced errors by me: 6

Number of giant spike balls: 6

Number of TKOs: 8

My final TKO rating, out of 8: 0

Number of times I wanted to smack Kevin Hart with a Frisboom: Too many to count

After reviewing the footage of my inglorious run, I realized that I played the course all wrong. I ran out as quickly as I could at every zone to try to finish before Hart hit me with his best shots — but in most cases, I threw myself out of sync and made his job even easier. If you ever find yourself on the TKO course, don't be like me and let your adrenaline take control. Find your balance first, then focus on dodging and finishing as fast as you can.

By the end of the day, Hart knocked me on my ass eight times — but the only thing that hurt was my pride (especially since $50k wasn't on the line). Still, I just wanted one thing after leaving Zone 4 sopping wet: A shot to man the battle stations myself to give Hart a taste of his own medicine.
