# What Happened When This Guy Ran 1 Mile Every Day for 30 Days

For just about any activity, fitness YouTuber Laurie Shaw has a 30-day challenge. He's tried everything from 100 bicep curls every day for 30 days, intense CrossFit sessions every day for 30 days, and even 200 pushups every other day for 30 days. But for his latest video, rather than focusing on strength, he's grinding out miles to boost his speed. His goal: to run the mile as fast as he can, with an ultimate goal of running a 5:30 mile.

[Ran Mile Every Day](https://glp1.peppysun.com/blog/9645304850.html)

He has a rough time from the start on Day 1.

"I'm .5 miles in and I'm starting to get a stitch," he says. "Ah, my legs. My lungs are burning. Man, I'm so out of shape."

He clocks in at 7:12.

"A lot of room to improve. I'm so interested to see what I'm going to get on Day 30," he says. "I'm going to stay committed and I'm going to stay consistent and push myself every day, and try and knock that 7-minute down bit by bit."

On Day 2, he already does, and finishes in 6:34.

He keeps going, going all-out for each 1-mile run.

"It was brutal. I'd collapse to the ground every time," he says.

By Day 9, he's cut his time down to 6:19.

He keeps going, but notes the run never got easier.

"Another day, another mile. Some days I had slower times. But overall, I felt myself progressing," he says.

By Day 16, he's "feeling fitter running-wise than I ever have in my life." And on Day 19, he clocks in his fastest time yet, 5:48. On Day 24, he has a friend help pace him, and reaches another milestone of 5:38—the next day, he shaved a second down to 5:37.

However, he starts to get coughing fits and shortness of breath after his runs, which he's concerned about, and does some research. He assumes the response is due to a hyperactive response from the lungs due to his increased heart rate, along with exercising in cold air. It's not serious, and he presses on.

By Day 29, he still hasn't hit 5:30. On Day 30, he get 5:46.

"Damn, I cannot crack the 5:30 mile. It kills me. That's a shame," he says. "I can safely say that I've given it my best shot. I've certainly gotten faster and fitter than I was when I started, and I've knocked off 1:35 in a 30-day period."

Part of the reason Shaw might have struggled was that he didn't vary his training at all during the period.
