# Keep it Sleek: Your Wallet

[it Sleek Your Wallet](https://pepgen.cc/category/bkm1i833h)

For anyone who learned a little something in my previous post about how your jeans should fit, I have one more pearl of wisdom: the fit of your pants can be thrown off by more than excess fabric. A huge wallet in your back pocket can also ruin an excellent look. Nothing interferes with a sleek profile or a woman's ability to assess your (ahem) assets, than an overstuffed wallet. Plus, when you whip it out on a date, women may interpret a messy, overflowing wallet as a reflection of your disorganized life. Seriously. (Of course, this isn’t always true, but I’d like to point to my husband as Exhibit A.)

Still not convinced? Not only does a huge wallet drag your jeans down when you wear it, but it can also stretch out the fabric of your pants. When you remove the bloated leather accessory after a few dozen wears, there will be a floppy empty space in its place. Ever notice this area is often the spot that wears away first on your jeans?

Changing is simple. Open your wallet and think about what you've used in the last 2 weeks -- which credit cards, receipts, business cards, etc, can you absolutely not do without? Be honest. Take those out and put them in a pile. I suggest carrying no more than two or three credit cards (one debit, one corporate or regular credit card -- and if you absolutely MUST, one emergency card).

Look at the other receipts, credit cards, gift cards, business cards, scraps of paper and whatever else you may be toting around and weed through it. Find a drawer to neatly stow these items. Try this new system out for a few days and adjust as needed.

If you can't curb your gathering instincts, I suggest finding a slimmer wallet or credit card case that will only let you carry the essentials. I like the options from a brand called Güs. The sleek Horizontal and Vertical wallets are exceptionally cool. For guys who really want to pare down their lives, the stainless steel money clip is a challenge worth taking.
