You can do all the curls you want for your biceps, but if you want them to really stand out, you want to spend extra time zeroing in on your biceps peaks. Most guys don't just want a set of simply big arms, but they want targeted definition. They want that baseball-sized look in their biceps when they flex.

And that takes focus when you biceps curl, which is what you'll work on in this move from Men's Health fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. Samuel's latest biceps-focused move is all about attacking your biceps peak, forcing you to get a great squeeze on every rep. "The top of the biceps curl can be a key moment to really grow and squeeze your biceps," says Samuel, "but oftentimes it's not. It's easy to get sloppy here."

You'll cheat on your biceps curl in one of three ways at the top, typically. Some people create so much momentum from the start of the curl that they carry that cheating into the end of the curl. Sometimes, people translate their elbows forward, taking stress off their biceps and putting it on their shoulders. There's a third cheat, too, says Samuel: Sometimes, people don't fully rotate their palms to face their shoulders. "That lack of supination leaves more stress on your brachialis than your biceps," says Samuel. "Both muscles are important, but we don't want to short-change our biceps focus at the top of the curl."

Samuel's 3D-Focused Biceps Peak Hellset prevents that from happening by stopping the curl midway through on the way up. "You're forced to check in with your body and be accountable for body positioning," says Samuel. "If you cheated to get things started, you'll have to correct that when you pause on the way up. Use the moment to get the most out of the curl."

You wind up being able to do a finite amount of reps, though, because the early time-under-tension and the focus on squeezes burns out your biceps faster than you think, says Samuel. That's why, after a solid workload of two-step curls early, you shift into regular curls. "The key to making this work," says Samuel, "is remembering what you learned on the two-step curls and the feeling of that early squeeze and carrying that into your regular curls."

It's a setup that'll add major biceps size and stress your biceps peaks too. You'll need a pair of dumbbells to do the 3D-Focused Biceps Peak Hellset; if you don't have one check out this adjustable pair from Bowflex.

Stand holding dumbbells at your sides, core tight and glutes squeezed. Curl both dumbbells upwards, turning your palms to face the ceiling. Stop the curl when your forearms are parallel with the ground. "Make sure your palms are facing the ceiling by this point," says Samuel.

Own the pause, then restart the curl, squeezing your biceps to lift the weights as high as possible. "Resist the temptation to rock at the waist and abs," says Samuel. "Focus on only squeezing your biceps." Lower back to the start. Do 6 to 8 reps like this.

Immediately transition into regular curl reps after those two-step reps. Do 6 to 8 regular curl reps per side.

That's 1 set. Do 3 sets.

The 3D-Focused Biceps Peak Hellset is a versatile move, says Samuel, that you can use in a variety of ways in your workout. You can do 3 sets as the lone biceps exercise you do on a pull day or a total body day. "Or make it your lead exercise on in a three-move biceps workout to really hammer your guns," says Samuel.

Either way, says Samuel, you'll want to take it lighter on the weight. "This is a challenging curl and form is paramount," he says. "So bury your pride and drop at least 5 pounds off your normal curling weight, at least to start. You'll work back up to a major weight after you own the form and technique."
