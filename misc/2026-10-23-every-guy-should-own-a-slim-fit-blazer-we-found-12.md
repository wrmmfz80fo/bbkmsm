What to Look for in a Slim-Fit Blazer

A slim-fit blazer can help you look slimmer. The ideal option should fit your body without being too tight. To ensure that you choose the right slim blazer, there's a few key elements to keep in mind.

Shoulder-to-Shoulder: The shoulder of the blazer should ends exactly where your shoulder points ends, according to Yang. This is the point where the shoulder meets the arm. If the blazer's shoulder is shorter than your shoulder points, it will pull and create a bite. If it's too wide, then you end up with shoulder sag. For those with broad shoulders, you may need to size up or it will create unsightly drape lines around your biceps and armhole.

Snug Armole: The armhole of the jacket should sit snugly under your arms, providing a slight hug without any restriction.

Collar Gap: There should be no gap between the collar of your blazer and the collar of your shirt. Your blazer should lay clean and flat against the back of your neck.

Layering Potential: When thinking of what to coordinate with the blazer, be mindful of the types of shirts and sweaters you're matching with your blazer, according to Craig Robinson, head of House and Master Tailor of Robinson Brooklyn.

Versatility: You want to check for something that can adapt to multiple settings, meaning it can be worn in a professional setting as well as out on the town.

What's the Difference Between a Blazer, Sport Coat or Suit Jacket?

Both sport coats and suit jackets are characterized by some of the same aspects as the blazer, but have different focuses. Blazers are usually distinguished by refined, neutral colors like black, grey or navy. They're also more structured and incredibly versatile, meaning they can be dressed up or down depending on your preference.

Sport coats often play up pops of color and patterned designs, including checks (plaids), herringbone, houndstooth and pinstripes. Like blazers, sport coats have transcended beyond their formality roots into casual style. Think: date night, intimate dinners or happy hour. Suit jackets are lighter in weight and look their best when paired with a matching pant. They're not meant to be worn with jeans or for casual wear, rather reserved for more celebratory or formal occasions such as job interviews, office presentations, or award ceremonies.
