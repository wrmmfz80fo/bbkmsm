The Arnold Strongman Classic is the heaviest strength competition of the year, bringing in competitors from around the globe to lift back-breaking objects like the Apollon’s Wheel and the Elephant Bar. We’ve been focusing a lot on the freakishly strong and occasionally frightening training regime of Iceland’s Hafþór Júlíus Björnsson — better known as The Mountain on Game of Thrones — but some of the other competitors who haven't crushed a man’s skull on a hit HBO drama aren’t as well known outside the world of competitive lifting.

Here are five strongmen to keep an eye on at this year's competition.

Brian Shaw (USA)

The four-time World's Strongest Man from the United States is the reigning champion from last year's Arnold Strongman Classic, narrowly beating out Björnsson himself. (The Mountain is very not chill about second-place finishes, FYI). Shaw was relatively quiet about his training on social media until last week, when he posted the above video of the "heaviest deadlift session ever," complete with a 1000-pound deadlift.

Mikhail Shivlyakov (Russia)

A former powerlifter and Navy marine, Shivlyakov transitioned to strongman competitions with a win in the 2013 Arnold Amateur Strongman. The 6'2" Russian has been training for the "bag over bar" competition by launching 91-pound bags 15 feet into the air.

Jerry Pritchett (USA)

USA's Jerry Pritchett — who also works as a metal fabricator and builds the majority of his own equipment — managed third place behind Björnsson and Shaw last year. But the Arizona native also nabbed a world record on the Elephant Bar with a 1,031 deadlift. (That record still stands.)

Mateusz Kieliskowski (Poland)

This 302-pounder out of Poland is the youngest competitor to ever compete in the Arnold Strongman Classic, winning the 2014 Arnold Amateur Strongman at just 21 years old. Before heading to Columbus, the now-24 year old showed off a 925-pound session.

Dimitar Savatinov (Bulgaria)

Bulgaria's Savatinov — an actual former circus strongman who travelled with Ringling Brothers for years — is a world record holder in the one-arm press, putting up a ridiculous 315 pounds last year. With the Strongman Classic a week away, Savatinov decided to showcase what he does best but "hates the most," a little bit of last minute cardio.
