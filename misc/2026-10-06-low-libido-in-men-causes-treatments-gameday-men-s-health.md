Causes of Low Libido in Men

Low libido is rarely the result of a single issue. More often, it’s a combination of hormonal, psychological, medical, and lifestyle factors that influence sexual desire. Understanding these causes is the first step toward finding an effective solution.

Hormonal Causes

Low testosterone is one of the most common biological contributors to low sex drive in men. Testosterone plays a key role in regulating libido, mood, muscle mass, and energy levels. When levels drop, so can your desire for sex. Adult males are considered to have low T if they fall below 300 nanograms per deciliter. (3)Other hormonal imbalances—such as elevated cortisol (the stress hormone) or thyroid dysfunction—may also play a role in reduced libido.

Psychological Factors

Mental health can also play a substantial role in sexual desire. Men dealing with stress, anxiety, or depression often experience a decrease in libido. A low sex drive is often a key symptom health practitioners look at when diagnosing depression. (4)Relationship challenges, lack of emotional intimacy, or performance anxiety can also interfere with arousal and desire.

Medical Conditions And Medications

Certain chronic illnesses—such as diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, or obesity—can lower libido directly or through the medications used to treat them. Medications that can lead to low libido include selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), blood pressure medications, certain H2 blockers, heart failure medications, opioids, and some cancer treatments. Consult your doctor if you suspect a medication may be affecting your sex drive.

Lifestyle Contributors

Sleep deprivation, excessive alcohol or drug use, lack of exercise, and poor nutrition can all impact hormone levels and blood flow, making it harder to maintain a healthy sex drive.

Obesity, in particular, is strongly linked to hormonal disruptions like low testosterone. Studies have shown that obese men are nearly nine times as likely to suffer from hypogonadism (low T) than non-obese men. (5)

Libido is about more than just physical arousal; it also reflects mental engagement and desire, which are indicators of a healthy sex drive. When men’s libido is low, it can manifest in several ways.
